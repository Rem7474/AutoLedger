# Developer reference

Detail behind the short rules in [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md). Read the section that matches the code being changed.

## Vehicle model

A vehicle has a `Powertrain` (`EV`, `ICE`, `PHEV` or `REEV`; plug-in hybrids and range-extenders record both charges and fill-ups, see the capability helpers in `models_vehicle.go`) and a `telemetry_mode` (`CONNECTED`, `SEMI_AUTO`, `MANUAL`). Vehicles also carry optional free-form `make` and `model` text attributes (`VARCHAR(100)`). ICE vehicles are tracked through fuel logs and have no TeslaMate link. EV vehicles may or may not have a TeslaMate connection (`teslamate_api_url`, `teslamate_car_id`, credentials stored encrypted) or can be tracked semi-automatically (CSV imports) or fully declaratively. TeslaMate-fed features (continuous telemetry sync, battery and temperature panels, sync button) are conditioned on `hasTeslaMate` (`web/src/utils/vehicles.ts`, exposed by the vehicle store): electric and a teslamateapi URL set. Non-Tesla and manual EV vehicles can log drives, import CSV files, and track expenses without requiring TeslaMate.

### Vehicle capabilities

Every vehicle returned by `GET /api/vehicles`, `GET /api/vehicles/{id}` and by the create and update routes carries `capabilities`, an object of booleans computed on each read by `models.ComputeCapabilities` (never stored, never accepted from a client). It is additive: `powertrain`, `telemetry_mode` and `toll_detection_active` stay. The integration routes (`/api/integrations/v1`) and the MCP tools have their own views and do not carry it. Clients read a capability instead of comparing the powertrain or testing for a connection.

| Capability | Rule |
| :--- | :--- |
| `charges` | powertrain other than `ICE` |
| `refuels` | `ICE`, `PHEV` or `REEV` (a plug-in hybrid has both) |
| `drives` | every vehicle (trips can be entered by hand or written by a source) |
| `sync` | a rechargeable vehicle with a teslamateapi URL; true for every role, though only the owner receives the URL |
| `odometer_auto` | `sync`, or at least one odometer reading reported by a source (`HA`) |
| `battery_health` | `charges` (readings can be recorded by hand or come from a source) |
| `tolls_auto` | `toll_detection_active` |
| `write` | role `OWNER` or `EDITOR` |

`FillVehicleCapabilities` (`internal/database/repository_vehicle_capabilities.go`) reads the stored facts (an odometer reading reported by a source) for a set of vehicles with one query and runs before the connection is hidden from a member who is not the owner.

## Manual drives

Every vehicle enters drives by hand, a combustion one included, so Trips, trip groups and carpools exist for every powertrain (`drives` is always true). Manual drives (`is_manual`) carry no energy cost. A vehicle that burns fuel and cannot charge has no kWh on a drive: the form has no kWh field, a typed or imported kWh is ignored (`ingest.DriveInput.FuelOnly`) and `energy_consumed_kwh` stays null. Its energy is fuel in litres: `fuel_liters` is the drive distance times the L/100 km of the fill-ups (full-to-full segments, all time), and the cost is those litres times the average price per litre (`fuel_rate_per_liter`). Without a fill-up pair the litres and the cost stay null (`UNKNOWN`), never an electricity price. The legacy `electricity_cost` field carries that fuel cost, and `fuel_only` tells a client to label it as fuel. A hybrid charges, so its drives stay on kWh with no fuel part. When no energy is typed, the server derives it from the vehicle's average consumption (`services.EstimateDriveEnergy`) and sets `energy_estimated`; the energy statistics ignore estimated energy. In the TCO mileage smoothing, tracking starts with the first synchronized drive or charge (the first manual charge without any), never with a manual drive, and a manual drive's distance still has its energy estimated before tracking starts.

## TeslaMate connection and sync

A vehicle's TeslaMate connection is a row of `vehicle_data_sources` (provider `TESLAMATE`, one per vehicle; non-secret settings in `config`, the bearer token in `secret_encrypted`, the basic-auth password in `basic_secret_encrypted`, both encrypted with the application key). The repository (`repository_vehicle_sources.go`) reads the source into the `Vehicle.TeslaMate*` fields and writes it on `CreateVehicle`/`UpdateVehicle`; `vehicles` has no TeslaMate column; code outside the repository only sees the `Vehicle` fields. Push channels (Home Assistant, scripts) are not sources.

TeslaMate sync (`internal/services/sync_*.go`): per-vehicle background jobs, resumable full import, a 30-day sliding re-read, reconciliation of deleted drives/charges, and a per-vehicle circuit breaker. Changing what is read from TeslaMate means resetting `sync_state.full_import_completed_at` in a migration so the next sync re-reads the history. A synchronized drive's consumption is its `energy_consumed_net` over its distance in km (`ingest.Consumption100km`), never TeslaMate's own `consumption_net`, which is in Wh per distance unit.

teslamateapi facts (verified in its source, its README has no field docs): drives/charges carry `battery_details{start_battery_level,end_battery_level}` and `outside_temp_avg` in `units.unit_of_temperature` (F is possible); `charge_energy_used` is `GREATEST(used, added)`, so used == added means "not measured" (DC); missing levels arrive as 0; `/battery-health` reports current capacity over the best capacity ever observed.

TeslaMate links: the vehicle's `teslamate_grafana_url` (Grafana) opens a drive or a charge in the standard dashboards (`teslamateDriveUrl`, `teslamateChargeUrl`; uids `zm7wN6Zgz` and `BHhxFeZRz`), and `teslamate_url` (the TeslaMate web application, `config.web_url` of the source) opens `/charge-cost/<teslamate_charge_id>`, where TeslaMate sets a charge's cost (`web/src/utils/teslamateCharges.ts`). Nothing is written to TeslaMate: a cost set there is read by the next synchronisation, unless the charge already carries a cost typed in AutoLedger (`cost_source = 'MANUAL'`). TeslaMateApi reports a cost that is not set as `0`, like a charge that really was free. A synchronised charge (`origin` and `cost_source` both `TESLAMATE`) priced 0 is therefore *to review* until the owner confirms it with `PATCH /vehicles/{id}/charges/{chargeId}/cost-review` (`charge_logs.cost_reviewed_at`, never touched by the synchronisation), as a drive is reviewed without toll. `database.ChargeCostToReviewSQL` (unknown cost, or a 0 not confirmed) is the one predicate behind `cost_to_review` on a charge, `charges_without_cost`, the "missing cost" filter, the completeness gaps and the average prices; the place statistics count a confirmed 0 as a free session and an unconfirmed one as unknown. The cost of a synchronised charge is set in TeslaMate while the synchronisation re-reads it: `teslamateCostEditUrl` returns the link for a charge of origin `TESLAMATE`, not `MANUAL`, that started within the 30 days re-read (`resyncOverlap`), on a vehicle with a `teslamate_url`. For such a charge the first action of the card links to TeslaMate and the charge form shows the cost read-only. Without that link (no URL, older charge, cost already typed in AutoLedger) the cost is entered in AutoLedger. `Repository.UpdateCharge` keeps `cost_source = 'TESLAMATE'` when a synchronised charge is saved with the cost and currency it already has (notes, place, receipt); a different cost sets `MANUAL`.

## Stored secrets

The TeslaMate credentials (`vehicle_data_sources.secret_encrypted`, `basic_secret_encrypted`) are the only values encrypted at rest (`internal/crypto`). A value is `v2:` followed by base64 of `salt (16 bytes) || nonce || AES-256-GCM(sealed)`. The AES key is derived from `AUTOLEDGER_ENCRYPTION_KEY` and the value's own random salt with Argon2id (2 passes, 19 MiB, one lane), after a purpose label (`autoledger/secrets-at-rest/v2`) prepended to the secret; the version and the label are authenticated as additional data. Derived keys are cached by salt, so a sync that decrypts the same credentials every cycle pays the derivation once.

A value without the prefix is the earlier form: base64 of `nonce || sealed`, under the SHA-256 of the secret. `Decrypt` reads both forms, `Encrypt` writes only `v2:`. At every start `rotateStoredSecrets` (`cmd/server/main.go`) rewrites the earlier form through `Repository.RotateTeslaMateSecrets`: a row is updated only while it still holds the values that were read, a value that cannot be read with the current secret is left as it is and counted in the log (`unreadable_sources`), and a second pass finds nothing to do. Another stored secret goes through the same `Encryptor` and is added to that pass.

A release that predates the `v2:` form cannot read the rewritten values, so going back to it needs the credentials entered again.

The secret is not changed in place: a different `AUTOLEDGER_ENCRYPTION_KEY` makes the stored credentials unreadable until they are entered again. A secret under 32 characters logs a warning at start (`Config.WeakEncryptionKey`); it never blocks the start.

## Maintenance reminder webhooks

`NotificationService.CheckAndNotify` evaluates one vehicle's reminders at a given odometer and posts the vehicle's webhook for each due one, at most once per 7 days unless the odometer moved by 500 km. It runs from two places: the TeslaMate sync, right after it recorded a higher odometer, and `runReminderChecks` (`cmd/server/workers.go`), which calls `CheckAllVehicles` at start-up and then every hour for every vehicle with an enabled webhook, at the odometer recorded for it. The scheduled run is what notifies a Home Assistant, manual or combustion vehicle, and a reminder that reaches its due date while the vehicle is parked.

## Home Assistant charging events

Charging sessions pushed by Home Assistant or a script (`POST /api/integrations/homeassistant/event`, `homeassistant_handler.go`) carry an optional `event_id`, stored as `external_id`: a session already recorded (same `event_id`, or without one a start within 30 min and an energy within 0.5 kWh), or still pending, is answered `200 duplicate`. A session without a vehicle goes to the home charger's default vehicle, otherwise to the only electric vehicle the account can edit, otherwise to `pending_charges` (a charger shared by several vehicles); assigning a pending charge is one transaction (`AssignPendingCharge`) and keeps the event's cost, battery levels and `event_id`. An odometer reading must name its vehicle. Nothing generated is stored as text (no note, no default location).

## Charge provenance

A charge carries its `origin` (`TESLAMATE`, `WEBHOOK`, `CSV`, `MANUAL`) and its `cost_source`. The charge modal and the charge list read the origin (`web/src/utils/chargeProvenance.ts`): the facts a TeslaMate synchronisation reports (start, end, energy, place, odometer) are read-only there and pinned by `UpdateCharge`; the cost, notes and receipt are AutoLedger's and survive resynchronisations. Charges pushed by Home Assistant or a script are `is_manual` rows with origin `WEBHOOK`, fully editable and deletable, since no synchronisation overwrites them.

## Errors and server-built text

A user-facing error is an `*apierror.Error` (`internal/apierror`): a stable dotted code (`vehicle.not_found`), an English message and, for formatted messages (`apierror.Newf`), the values as `p0`, `p1`, ... in verb order. Answer with `writeAPIError`, or `writeErr` for an error that may carry one; the JSON body is `{error, code, params}`. Non-failure server-built text (sync warnings, comparison assumptions, tire wear explanations) uses the same shape through `apierror.Message` (`= Error`) and `NewMessage`/`NewMessagef`, embedded as a struct field and serialized as `{code, message, params}`. Each code needs an entry in `web/src/locales/{en,fr}/errors.json` (failures) or `messages.json` (non-failures) — a front-end test lists the codes used in the Go source — with `{p0}` placeholders. Internal failures use the code `internal` and log the cause. Never put French text in Go source.

Text with no HTTP request to translate through (reminder and sync-failure webhooks, sent from a background job) or that ends up stored as plain text rather than a code (auto-generated toll notes, a trip's default name) uses `internal/servertext` instead: `servertext.Text(lang, "reminder.discord_title", args...)`, catalog and both languages kept in one file. `lang` is the acting/owning user's stored `Language` ("en"/"fr", column added by `000032_user_language`); resolve it with `requestLanguage(r, repo)` in a handler or a small local `language(ctx, userID)` method in a service that already holds `*database.Repository`. The UI's own language stays client-side (`web/src/i18n`, `localStorage`); `PUT /api/auth/language` only updates this stored copy, called by `LanguageSwitcher.vue` and once after registration.

## Concurrent edits

An item that several people or tools can edit carries a `version` column (`BIGINT NOT NULL DEFAULT 1`). Every write made by a user (REST `PUT`, MCP tool, freeze or unfreeze) runs `version = version + 1`; background jobs (sync, geocoding, linked-cost refresh) never advance it, so they cannot raise a conflict.

- Versioned items: carpools, vehicles, manual drives, charges, fill-ups, maintenance expenses, drive expenses, odometer readings, trip groups, tires, tire mount sessions, tread measurements, reminders, tariff plans, comparison scenarios and the acquisition contract. A partial write (drive tags, driver or toll review, the estimated energy, a tire disposal or rotation, completing a reminder) advances the version without checking one. Single-field settings are exempt (see the route-table test).
- Responses expose `version`. A `PUT` accepts `expected_version` in the body (the offline queue replays the body, not headers). Absent: the write goes through. Present and different from the stored version: `409` with the code `request.stale`; present for an item that does not exist: `404`.
- The check sits in the `UPDATE ... WHERE (expected IS NULL OR version = expected)` itself, so no write can slip between the test and the write. On zero rows, `staleOrNotFound` (`internal/database/versioning.go`) tells a missing item (`ErrNotFound`) from a changed one (`ErrStaleWrite`); the table must be in `versionedTables`. `writeRepoError` maps `ErrStaleWrite` to `request.stale`.
- An MCP tool that changes an item requires `expected_version`, read from the matching `get_*` tool; a preview (`dry_run`) compares in memory, a real save is guarded by the SQL.
- The SPA turns the `409` into a `StaleWriteError` (`services/api.ts`); a modal calls `useStaleWrite().explainStaleWrite(err)`, which shows one alert, then closes the modal and reloads the list. A modal sends `expected_version: props.editing.version`.
- `TestEveryUpdateRouteIsVersionedOrListed` (`cmd/server/routes_test.go`) fails for a `PUT` or `PATCH` route that is neither versioned nor exempt with a reason. A route that edits an item goes in the first list as soon as it has a `version`.

## Action menu

`web/src/components/ActionMenu.vue` is the "⋯" menu of one card or one page: a vehicle card (setup, sharing, data sources, delete) and the import/export of the odometer page. It is not for list rows: a list row is one tappable card whose actions live in its detail and in the selection bar (`BulkSelectionBar`). Entries are `.menu-item` buttons or links in the default slot, the destructive one last with `menu-item-danger`; a click on an entry closes the menu. On a wide screen the menu is a popover under the button. On a phone it is a bottom sheet (`ModalShell`) titled with the `heading` prop, with entries at least 48px high and pulled down to close. The `phone` prop keeps the button visible below `sm`; without it the button is desktop only. An action that exists as a visible button on the card (the vehicle's Edit pencil) is not repeated as an entry. `ActionMenu.test.ts` lists the files allowed to import the component.

## Screen budget

Each screen stays inside a fixed budget. The ceilings are a review criterion: a PR that changes a screen states the count before and after for the criteria it touches and shows the screen at 390 px.

| Criterion | Ceiling | How it is checked |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Lead figure | 1 per screen | `colourRules.test.ts` allows one `text-hero` per file |
| Chart visible without an action | 1 | PR captures |
| Page length outside the list | 2 screens (about 1,700 px at 390 px, the list itself excluded) | PR captures |
| Pieces of information per list row | 4 | PR captures |
| Chips per list row | 1, for a deviation | PR captures (a pill is clickable only, see `StatusLabel`) |
| Permanent help texts | 1 per page | PR captures: every other explanation is a `GlossaryTip` |
| Filter rows | 1 | PR captures |

A permanent help text is a sentence in `text-slate-400` or `text-slate-500` under a title or a figure that stays on screen. Information about a term, a figure or a chart goes in `glossary.json` and is read through `GlossaryTip`. A text that tells the user what to do next (an empty state, a validation message, a field hint inside a form) is not a help text of the page.

No script measures page height or chips per row. Both depend on the amount of data on the screen, so a count taken on seeded data does not hold on another account and a threshold test would fail without a regression or pass with one. Screen length and chip count are read on the captures, with the same data before and after.

### Figures

A block of figures has three levels. `KpiCard` with `lead` is the one figure the block is read for. `FigureSecondary` (label, value, optional unit and `GlossaryTip`) carries two or three more, frameless, in a two or three column grid. Everything else is in the detail of the block. A figure whose value is unknown is not rendered; `keep-empty` is for a figure whose absence is itself the message and which links to what fills it.

### Tabs

`TabBar` is the only tab component: a `tablist` with roving focus, a selected tab in the rose look, icon and label. It takes no counter or badge; a count belongs in the content of the tab. `CarpoolPassengers` keeps its own tablist because it picks a passenger inside a form. `disclosureRules.test.ts` lists both.

### Disclosures

`<details>` holds the advanced inputs of a form (maintenance, CSV import, what-if, resale assumptions, battery reading) and nothing else. Content that matters to the page is shown, not folded. The files allowed to use it are listed in `web/src/utils/disclosureRules.test.ts`, and the test fails for any other file.

## SonarCloud coverage

Coverage for SonarCloud: CI writes `coverage.out` (`go test -coverprofile -coverpkg=./...`, PostgreSQL-backed, so repositories count) and `web/coverage/lcov.info` (`npm run test:coverage`, scope `utils`, `composables`, `stores`), uploads them as artifacts and the `SonarCloud Scan` job passes them to Sonar through `sonar-project.properties` (`sonar.go.coverage.reportPaths`, `sonar.javascript.lcov.reportPaths`). The job runs only when the repository variable `SONAR_CI_ANALYSIS` is `true` and the secret `SONAR_TOKEN` exists, and requires SonarCloud's automatic analysis to be switched off; Dependabot and fork PRs are not scanned; the CI jobs that need no secret (tests, coverage, build) still run on them, and SonarCloud analyses `main` once they are merged.

## Frontend i18n

Every user-visible string goes through vue-i18n (English and French). Catalogs are `web/src/locales/<lang>/<namespace>.json`; the message key is `<namespace>.<path>`. Templates use `$t('ns.key', { param })`, scripts, stores and utils use `t` from `@/i18n` (call it while rendering so it follows the language). Plurals use the `singular | plural` form with `t(key, count)`. Escape `@ | { } $` in a message as `{'@'}`. Add a key to both languages: `i18n.test.ts` fails when the key sets or placeholders differ. Shared wording (`Cancel`, `Save`, ...) lives in the `common` namespace. A word that sits between two values ("at 45,210 km", "in September", "From"/"To") is part of the message too, with the values as placeholders; when one value needs its own markup, use `<i18n-t keypath="...">` with a named slot rather than splitting the sentence. Input examples use `common.example` (`ex : 850` / `e.g. 850`), decimals through `$n()`. Dates and numbers use `intlLocale()`, never a hard-coded `fr-FR`. The product name (AutoLedger) is `APP_NAME` in `web/src/brand.ts`; the module path, image, database and volume names keep the original `teslacost` identifiers.

## Distance, fuel volume and consumption units

Distances are always stored, computed and sent to the API in kilometers; `web/src/units.ts` converts for display (`formatDistance(km)`) and form input (`displayDistanceToKm(value)`) based on the signed-in account's stored `distance_unit` ("km"/"mi", column added by `000033_user_distance_unit`, set with `PUT /api/auth/distance-unit`). New UI that shows or accepts a distance goes through it rather than hard-coding "km": `formatDistance`/`formatDistanceValue` for a distance, `perDistance`/`formatPerDistanceValue` for a figure per km or per 100 km (a cost per km, kWh/100 km: per mile it is 1.609 times larger), `speedUnit`/`formatSpeed` for a speed, and `<DistanceInput>` (`web/src/components/DistanceInput.vue`) for a form field, which keeps its model in km (`kind="per-distance"` for a figure per km, `whole` when the API field is an integer, `text` when the form keeps the field as a string). Catalog strings take the unit through `{unit}` (`"Odometer ({unit})"`, `"{cost}/{unit}"`) and `i18n.test.ts` fails when a call forgets `unit` (or `cur`, `speed`, `min`, `band`) for a message that needs it. Figures per km that the API computes (`cost_per_km`, `consumption_kwh_100km`) are converted on the frontend with `perDistance`; the API itself stays metric. A drive reported without a distance (an integration with no odometer) has a `NULL` `drives.distance_km`, `Drive.DistanceKm` is nil and the API sends `distance_km: null`; Go code that needs a number calls `Drive.Distance()` (0 when unknown), writers use `ingest.KnownDistance` (nil for 0), SQL that scans a drive distance into a non-pointer wraps it in `COALESCE`, and the frontend's `formatDistance`/`formatDistanceValue` show `null` as a dash. A figure per km computed over a distance the vehicle does not have yet is `null` (the TCO `*_cost_per_km` fields, the fleet `running_cost_per_km` and `full_cost_per_km`, a month's `cost_per_km` when it has distance but no cost): the frontend shows it as a dash through `formatAmountOrUnknown`, and a chart leaves a gap, never a point at zero. Fuel volumes follow the same rule with the account's `volume_unit` ("l"/"gal_us"/"gal_uk", `000059_user_volume_unit`, `PUT /api/auth/volume-unit`): stored and sent in litres, shown through `formatVolume`/`volumeUnitLabel`/`perVolume` and typed in `<DistanceInput kind="volume" | "per-volume">`. A fuel consumption (stored as L/100 km) goes through `formatFuelConsumptionValue` + `fuelConsumptionUnit` and `<DistanceInput kind="consumption">`: it reads as mpg (US or UK) for miles with gallons, otherwise as a volume per 100 distance units. CSV files, the ingestion API and server-built error texts stay in litres. A distance inside an API error or message is passed to `apierror.Newf` as `apierror.Km(v)` (a figure per km as `apierror.PerKm(v)`): the English text formats it like a float, the parameter reaches the client as `"km:<v>"` / `"perkm:<v>"`, and `apiErrorMessage` converts it and fills `{unit}`. Server-built text with no request (reminder webhooks) uses `servertext.Distance(unit, km)` with the owner's stored unit.

## Currency

Each vehicle has its own `Currency` (ISO 4217, `vehicles.currency`, migration `000034_vehicle_currency`), fixed at creation and never edited afterward — `UpdateVehicle`'s SQL simply has no `currency` column in its `SET`, so a value sent on update is silently ignored; existing (pre-migration) vehicles keep `EUR`. A stored money amount in another currency needs an `fx_rate` to the vehicle's own currency, same mechanism as before but relative to the vehicle instead of hard-coded to the euro (`normalizeCurrency(currency, baseCurrency, fxRate)` in `internal/handlers/validation.go`; SQL sites compare `currency = (SELECT currency FROM vehicles WHERE id = ...)` or join `vehicles v` and compare `v.currency`, never a literal `'EUR'` — `amountInVehicleCurrencyExpr` in `internal/database/repository_currency.go` is the shared helper). The toll dataset (French autoroutes, OpenTollData) is the one amount that stays euro-denominated regardless of the vehicle's currency. On the frontend, `web/src/currency.ts`'s `formatMoney(cents, currency)` / `formatAmount(amount, currency)` use `Intl.NumberFormat` (`currencySymbol(currency)` for a bare symbol, e.g. a chart axis); pass the vehicle's own `currency` (`vehicleStore.currency` for the active vehicle), never assume EUR. Catalog strings carry no currency sign: an amount placeholder receives an already formatted amount (`"Fair share due: {amount}"`, called with `formatAmount(...)`), and a field label takes the symbol through `{cur}` (`"Amount ({cur})"`, called with `currencySymbol(...)`), so the sign lands where the locale puts it (`12,00 €` vs `US$12.00`). A manual expense form's own currency field defaults to the vehicle's currency (never `'EUR'`) and calls `currencyPayload(form, baseCurrency)` from `web/src/utils/expenses.ts` to decide whether `fx_rate` is required — the same `baseCurrency` the backend validates against.

### Where an amount gets its currency

An amount never has an assumed currency. A write either carries its currency, takes the one of its vehicle, or is refused; the guards are `internal/currencyguard` (Go sources) and `web/src/currencyLiterals.test.ts` (frontend sources), which fail on a new `"EUR"` literal outside their short lists of reasons.

| Where | Currency | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `POST /vehicles` | `vehicles.currency` | Required, ISO 4217 code, fixed at creation. No default: the repository refuses a vehicle without one, and the column default only fills rows that predate the column. |
| Tariff plans, public charging presets | own `currency` column | Required and validated on create; validated when changed on a plan update. No default. |
| Comparison scenario in projection mode | `comparison_scenarios.currency` | Required. A request without one takes the currency shared by every vehicle of the account (a client that predates the field, an offline write queued before the update); when the vehicles differ or there are none, it is refused with `comparison.currency_required`. A retrospective scenario takes the currency of its vehicle. |
| Comparison form prefill (`GET /comparison-scenarios/defaults`) | none stored | The built-in prices are written for the euro only: they are returned for `currency=EUR` and dropped for any other currency or when none is given. |
| Charges and expenses entered by hand | `charge_logs.currency`, `drive_expenses.currency`, `maintenance_expenses.currency` | Optional on the request: the vehicle's currency when absent. Another currency needs an `fx_rate` to the vehicle's. |
| Fuel logs, tires, ownership (purchase, loan, lease), carpool trips, legs and payments | none: the vehicle's | These tables hold amounts in the currency of their vehicle, never another. |
| Automatic toll estimate | `drive_expenses.currency = 'EUR'` | The toll dataset is priced in euros. On a vehicle in another currency the expense has no `fx_rate`, so the vehicle-currency sums leave it out. |
| `users.fleet_monthly_budget` | `users.fleet_monthly_budget_currency` | Set in the currency of the first vehicle of the household, stored with the amount and returned as `budget_currency`. Setting a budget with no vehicle is refused. |
| Fleet summary without a vehicle | empty | `currency` is empty rather than a default. |
| Frontend formatting | `formatAmount(amount, currency)` | An empty currency (the vehicle is not loaded yet) formats as a plain number, never as euros. |

## Reporting months

Months are calendar months of the reporting timezone (`APP_TIMEZONE`) everywhere: the TCO monthly costs, the fleet summary, the instalments due later in the month and the current-month figures. The TCO and the fleet summary carry `current_month` (`YYYY-MM`), which the client uses instead of its own clock. `monthly_costs` is continuous: every month from the first record to the current one is present, a month without a record as an empty row with an unknown cost per kilometre, so a range of N months is N calendar months. `GetFleetSummary` takes the timezone name.

## Cost history

`GET /api/vehicles/{vehicleId}/cost-history?kind=tolls|service|fixed` (`TCOService.CostHistory`) answers what the vehicle paid per calendar month for the family of costs of one page: `tolls` is the travel expenses (`drive_expenses`: toll, parking, other), `service` the maintenance expenses of category maintenance, repair, accessory and other, `fixed` those of category insurance, subscription, tax and financing. It reads the `cost_ledger` rows of that source and those categories, so recurring expenses count once per due month and a foreign amount is converted with its own rate. Months are those of the reporting timezone and continuous from the first record to the current month (a later recorded month extends them), a month without a record is a zero. The cash view only: a smoothed cost (economic) is a property of the TCO monthly costs, not of this endpoint.

`GET /api/vehicles/{vehicleId}/cost-entries?from=&to=&category=&min_amount=&sort=&order=&limit=&offset=` (`TCOHandler.GetCostEntries`) lists the `cost_ledger` lines of a vehicle, newest first, with the record each comes from (`source` is the table, `source_id` its row, so a client can open, edit or delete it). `from` and `to` are days of the reporting timezone, both inclusive; `category` takes the lower-case ledger categories (comma separated or repeated); the acquisition is never a line. `entries` is one page of at most 200 (50 by default) and `next_offset` is the offset of the next page, null on the last one. `paid_total` and `by_category` add up every matching line, not only the page. `carpool_received` is what the passengers paid for the carpools dated in the period and `net_total` is `paid_total` minus it; `distance_km` is the distance of the drives that began in the period (the base of a cost per kilometre, drives without a recorded distance add nothing); with a `category` or `min_amount` filter the lines are not the whole cost, so `carpool_received` is 0. For a closed month, `paid_total` equals the TCO monthly `total` less its `smoothed_energy` (an estimate with no line) and `scheduled` (instalments not yet due), and `carpool_received` equals its `carpool_revenue`. The route is read-only and any member of the vehicle can call it.

`GET /api/vehicles/{vehicleId}/charges/{chargeId}`, `.../expenses/{expenseId}` and `.../maintenance/{maintenanceId}` read one record, in the shape of the matching list, so a line of `cost-entries` (its `source` is `charge_logs`, `drive_expenses` or `maintenance_expenses`, its `source_id` the id) can be opened in the form that edits it. Any member of the vehicle can read; an id that belongs to another vehicle, or that is not an id, answers 404, as does a charge deleted upstream.

The client (`CostHistory.vue`, `utils/costHistory.ts`) draws the bars of the months of the chosen period (a preset counted in calendar months, the current one included, or the months a range of days covers entirely), the total and monthly average of that period, and the 12-month average: for each month, the average of that month and the eleven before it, over the months that exist while the history is shorter than twelve. The lists of tolls, maintenance and fixed costs sit below it and keep their own filters; none of them shows a total over everything.

## Energy cost per 100 km

The cost per 100 km of energy has one definition: `EnergyStats.Summary.CostPer100km` (priced charges over the distance of the months where distance is tracked, at least 50 km). The fleet summary (`energy_cost_per_100km`) and the Home Assistant vehicle metrics read it from `EnergyStatsService`; the TCO card shows the amount, names the estimated part and links to the energy panel instead of a second per-distance figure. A vehicle without a figure reports 0 in the fleet.

The charging statistics (`charging-place-stats`) report the energy of the distance driven before tracking started next to the recorded sessions: `summary.estimated_kwh`, `estimated_cost` and `estimated_km`, and `estimated_kwh` / `estimated_cost` on each month of `series`. It is the figure the TCO adds to its energy (`TCOService.EstimatedEnergyByMonth`, the vehicle's average consumption and price over the pre-tracking distance of each month), kept out of the places, the sessions and the price per kWh. A period of whole months takes the months it reaches; a custom range of days takes only the months it covers entirely. The charges tab adds the estimate to the energy and spent figures and shows it as its own bar in the monthly chart.

The fleet summary never adds two currencies. `currency` is the one of the first vehicle and is the currency of `current_month_cost` and the monthly chart, which count only the vehicles in it. The household budget has its own `budget_currency`; the page compares it with `current_month_cost` only while the two are equal, and otherwise shows the budget as stale (an amount is never converted). The note under the budget gives the number of vehicles left out of the figure. `totals` has one line per currency (`FleetCurrencyTotals`) with the month cost and the number of vehicles. The comparison table and the cheapest/most economical badges rank within one currency (`web/src/utils/fleetRanking.ts`). Amounts are never converted: there is no exchange-rate source. The distance of a vehicle and of the household, for the month in progress and for the history, is the monthly distance of each vehicle's TCO (drives plus readings and fill-ups), so a combustion or manual vehicle counts. `energy_cost_per_100km` is the energy-statistics figure and, for a vehicle those statistics do not cover (fuel only), the energy cost of its TCO over its distance basis; it is `null` when unknown and 0 only when the cost is known to be zero. The household view is the home page with `?scope=all` (`/fleet` redirects to it): `DashboardView` reads the scope with `scopeFromQuery` (`utils/vehicleScope.ts`, `all` only when the account has at least two vehicles) and renders `components/fleet/FleetOverview.vue` (hero with one line per currency, then `FleetBudget`, `FleetComparison` and `FleetTrend`) or `components/dashboard/VehicleToday.vue`. The scope lives in the URL, so a link and a reload keep it; the top bar selector offers "All vehicles" only on the home page, and choosing a vehicle there leaves the household view. The view has one comparison table (cards are not repeated) and no energy-only badge.

The completeness score (`completenessScore`, `tco.completeness`) weighs seven dimensions and never counts an empty one as complete: a dimension with nothing to evaluate (no motorway drive, no odometer reading to compare, no priced expense) is `applicable: false` and its weight goes to the others, and a vehicle with no charge scores 0 on energy. `score_available` is false while the vehicle has no distance and no expense; `score_pct` is then 0 and the client shows no score. A bought vehicle whose depreciation is unknown is capped at `depreciationMissingCap`, one point under the ranking threshold. Each dimension carries a `label_key` translated on the client (`dashboard.dataQualityCard.dimensions.*`), `label` is an English fallback, and `acquisition_missing_fields` (`purchase_price`, `resale_value`, `holding_period`) lets the card name what to enter.

`completeness.gaps` (`completenessGaps`) lists what is missing with its worth, largest first: the missing depreciation (the largest item of a bought vehicle, never estimated), then the estimated amounts in descending order (unpriced kWh at the vehicle's own average price per kWh, untracked distance at its own energy cost per km), then the gaps with no amount (unconverted foreign amounts). The dashboard card orders its actions by that list and labels each amount as an order of magnitude; nothing is filled in automatically.

The home page is the page of the present (`DashboardView`). `TodayHero` shows the cash paid so far in the current month, net of carpool revenue, the part of the month already scheduled, a bar of the month's themes and one cost per distance on the same basis (`todayHero`, built on `buildMonthBreakdown` in cash mode). `UpcomingList` lists the overdue and due-soon reminders, the next planned one and the most worn tire once it is a warning (`upcomingItems`), or says nothing is planned. `DataQualityCard` (its entries-to-check lines come from `GET /vehicles/{id}/data-alerts`, computed per request in `services/data_alerts.go`, thresholds in [docs/data-alerts.md](../data-alerts.md)) renders only while the completeness is below 100 % or an entry looks mistyped. The monthly, cost-per-distance and breakdown charts, the Pro/Perso block (rendered only when at least one drive carries a tag) and the month detail are on the costs page; the totals since purchase, the battery, the resale value and the lease contract are on the vehicle sheet. The monthly cost chart is economic (each cost spread over the period it covers) or cash (`monthlyCostSeries`); distance and cost per distance are two charts with one axis each, and a month under `MIN_PLOTTED_DISTANCE_KM` (50 km) is not plotted on the cost chart.

The cost of a drive has one definition: `ComputeDriveCost` over the unit rates in force when the drive ended (`CarpoolService.DriveRates`, one batched `GetVehicleUnitRatesAtMany` call for any number of drives; a drive without an end time uses its start). The drive list, `tag_breakdown`, `EstimateCosts` and the carpool legs all go through it. The rates look at the charges, tire mounts and maintenance dated up to the reference only, so a price entered later for an earlier charge reaches the drive and a later charge does not. A carpool leg made from a drive takes that drive's distance and cost (`followDrive`), with the categories listed in its `cost_overrides` replaced by the amounts the user chose; `drive_cost` returns the drive's own cost next to it. The legs are refreshed on every read (`RefreshLinkedCosts`: a page of the carpool list, the detail, integration and MCP views), and the refreshed amounts and trip totals are written back so SQL aggregates match. `GET /vehicles/{id}/carpools` is paged (`page`, `limit`: 30 by default, 100 at most; `trip_group_id` keeps one journey) and only refreshes the page it returns; `GET /vehicles/{id}/carpools/summary` totals every carpool from the stored amounts, so a carpool whose page has not been read since its drive changed can lag until it is. A carpool with `costs_frozen_at` set keeps its stored amounts; `POST /carpools/{id}/freeze` and `/unfreeze` toggle it, and saving with `costs_frozen: true` stores the amounts as sent. A leg whose drive was deleted becomes a manual leg and keeps its last amounts. A trip entered by distance is priced by `EstimateCosts` when created or recalculated and is not recomputed on read.

`tag_breakdown` (`tagBreakdown`) splits the distance, the measured energy and the cost of the drives by tag. The cost of a drive is the one above, so the cost of a tag is the sum of the drives the list shows for that tag. A drive with several tags counts in each of them (`shared_drives`); costs are computed only once a drive is tagged, and the dashboard block renders only then.

A monthly row carries `scheduled`: the part of the month in progress's `total` (and `financing`) that falls due after today, from the loan and lease schedule. The dashboard shows `total - scheduled` as spent so far and `scheduled` as already scheduled; variable costs are not forecast.

## Calendar intervals and date boundaries

Use `models.AddCalendarMonths` for a monthly interval from an arbitrary day: 31 January plus one month ends on 28 February (29 in a leap year). `time.Time.AddDate` remains suitable for day increments and for month buckets explicitly starting on day 1. `internal/calendarcheck` checks production calls to `AddDate` with a nonzero or variable month argument in `internal/` and `cmd/`. Each reviewed first-of-month file is listed with its reason; a call in any other file, or a listed file that no longer adds months, fails the normal Go test suite. The guard is syntactic, not a proof of the receiver's value: a change to how a bucket start is built still needs review. It sees `AddDate` only: a month computed in SQL (`+ interval '1 month'`, which PostgreSQL clamps) or by adding a fixed number of days is outside its scope.

Keep a calendar date and an instant distinct. For a SQL `DATE` boundary, first derive the day in an explicit reporting timezone and bind that day; for `TIMESTAMPTZ`, compare instants. Do not rely on a database session's timezone to turn an instant into a day. A local day range ends at the next local midnight, not at midnight plus 24 hours (DST changes the day's duration).

### Days in the frontend

The server cuts days and months in its reporting timezone, and sends that zone as `reporting_timezone` with the user (`/api/auth/me`, login and registration answers). `web/src/utils/reportingTime.ts` holds it (`setReportingTimezone`, called by the auth store) and is the only place that turns "now" or a timestamp into a calendar day: `reportingToday()`, `dayKey(date)`, `monthKey(date)`, `reportingYear()`, `dayParts(date)`. Period bounds and presets, the default dates of the forms, the day and month headings of the journals, the month in progress and the file-name dates go through it, never through `getDate`, `getMonth`, `getFullYear` or `toISOString().slice` on an instant. Without a configured zone (signed out) the browser's zone is used. Timestamps are still displayed in the browser's zone; only the day a record belongs to follows the reporting zone. Arithmetic on a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` (next week, next month) is plain calendar arithmetic and stays on local `Date` components. The value of a `datetime-local` input is a browser wall-clock time converted to an instant by the browser.

## Phone navigation

The bottom bar has four tabs around the quick entry button: Home (`/`), Trips (`/drives`) or, for a vehicle with no trips (combustion), Consumption (`/consumption`), Expenses (`/costs`) and Vehicle (`/vehicle`); `phoneTabs` and `activePhoneTab` in `web/src/utils/navigation.ts` decide them, and a page keeps its tab lit (maintenance under Expenses, odometer, tires and comparison under Vehicle). There is no "more" entry. The trips page has a segment Drives | Trips | Carpools kept in the address (`?tab=TRIPS`, `?tab=CARPOOLS`); the carpools segment is `CarpoolView` embedded (a carpool opens from `?open=<id>`, a new one from `?new_drive_id`, `?new_drive_ids` or `?new_trip_group_id`), and it offers the latest drives no carpool covers. The consumption page (`/consumption`, `ExpensesView` in its consumption section) holds the tools of the energy of a vehicle: efficiency and estimate for an electric one, fill-ups for a combustion one, efficiency and fill-ups for a hybrid, and for each of them the charges whose cost is still to complete; the charge and fill-up lists are read on the Costs page. The fill-ups tab carries a period bar with its own period (`usePeriod('fuel')`, the current month at first, kept apart from the period of the Costs page and of the carpools): every fill-up is loaded once, the rows, month headers and the four figures follow the period, and the consumption of an interval stays the one the server measures over the whole history, counted in the period of the fill-up that closes it and weighted by distance (`fuelPeriodSummary` in `utils/fuelLogs.ts`). `/carpools` redirects to `/drives?tab=CARPOOLS`, and `/energy` to `/consumption` when its `tab` is a tool tab (`EFFICIENCY`, `ESTIMATE`, `FUEL`, `CHARGES`) or to `/costs?theme=energy` otherwise (`energyRedirect`); the query string is kept. The other pages are reached from the vehicle page (tiles for maintenance, consumption, tires, readings and comparison; the consumption tile shows kWh per 100 for a vehicle that charges, and litres per 100 with the cost of 100 km, from the fill-ups, for a combustion one), from the vehicle selector (all vehicles, manage vehicles, add a vehicle) and from the account sheet (`AccountSheet.vue`, opened by the profile button of the top bar: account page, language, sign out). The desktop sidebar lists every page in `NAV_SECTIONS`.

## Icons

Icons come from `web/src/icons/index.ts`, which exports Material Symbols (rounded, weight 400) under the names the screens use (`Car`, `Receipt`, `Gauge`, …). `defineIcon` turns a single-path SVG file of the `@material-symbols/svg-400` package (a development dependency: the paths are inlined at build time) into a component that draws in the current text colour, takes its size from its class and forwards its attributes. A screen imports its icon from `@/icons`; a new glyph is added there with its file name from the package, in one place. A test that checks an icon is absent looks for its `data-icon` attribute. `icons/defineIcon.test.ts` checks that every export draws a path.
